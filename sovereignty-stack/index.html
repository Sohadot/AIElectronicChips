Silicon Sovereignty Stack

The Silicon Sovereignty Stack maps the dependencies that make AI compute possible.

The stack is an analytical ontology, not a maturity ranking. It prevents a single capability from being mistaken for control of the complete compute substrate.

Reference framingThis page defines an analytical layer. It does not make a current operational claim about any entity.
00

Reference summary

Definition. The Silicon Sovereignty Stack is the public navigation view of Compute Sovereignty Ontology v1 and its eight dependency layers.

Compute sovereignty relationship. The stack prevents single-layer capability from being mistaken for full compute control. Every layer page maps to one row in the ontology.

Boundary. The stack is not a maturity ranking or sovereignty score. It does not make operational claims about any entity without scoped evidence.

Eight connected dependency layers

01

Read across layers

Each layer has its own evidence, constraints, suppliers, permissions, and substitutes. A credible assessment records these differences instead of collapsing them into one headline.

02

Read between layers

Exposure often appears at transitions: a design that needs unavailable fabrication, a package constrained by memory, or installed systems limited by power and cooling.

The cross-layer research pathways below follow three such transitions through the published evidence.

03

No universal score

The stack supports structured diagnosis and comparison within a declared scope. It does not produce an arbitrary universal sovereignty score.

04

Pathway A: HBM to advanced packaging

Research question. How do high-bandwidth memory architecture and advanced packaging interact in AI accelerator integration, and which industrial dependencies are actually documented?

Take a researcher from the HBM4 interface specification to the specific packaging evidence an integration claim would need, separating technology architecture from product-level qualification.

Layers
Memory, Fabrication, Packaging, Design
Technical scope
HBM4 and HBM4E stacks and interposer-class packaging as disclosed by JEDEC, memory suppliers, TSMC and AMD
Time
Sources dated 2022-03-02 to 2026-11-05; releases compiled 2026-11-09
Releases
aec-hbm4-evidence-release-001, aec-ide-003-advanced-packaging
Assumptions
Supplier and foundry statements describe their own products and plans, not third-party integration; The MI300X case is used because it is the only accelerator in the reviewed evidence with a disclosed package technology
