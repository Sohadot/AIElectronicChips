Evidence layer · Registry protocol and source boundaries

Source Registry

A governed index of official and high-quality sources that may support future reference pages, dependency records, briefs, and audits.

Registration records source identity, scope, freshness, and limitations. It does not approve every statement in a source or turn source material into a finding.

Evidence boundaryA registered source can support a scoped claim only after claim-specific review. Sources do not automatically create findings.
00

Reference summary

Definition. The source registry is the governed evidence index for compute sovereignty reference work.

Compute sovereignty relationship. Every sensitive claim must trace to a registered source with scope, limitations, and claim-specific review.

Boundary. Registry presence does not create a finding, ranking, recommendation, or inherited confidence. The Compute Sovereignty Standard requires evidence, boundaries, and confidence discipline; it does not rank companies, countries, or products.

01

Purpose

The registry preserves the metadata needed to assess whether a source is attributable, current enough, and relevant to a specific layer and claim type. It supports governed research; it is not a conclusions database.

02

Source hierarchy

Government rules, regulations, official notices, and official datasets are preferred where applicable. Company filings, annual reports, attributable official technical disclosures, technical standards, peer-reviewed research, and official institutional reports follow according to the claim being tested.

03

Registry status

Active means source identity and metadata have been reviewed. Pending review means useful material requires further verification before detailed claim use. Retired sources remain historical records and cannot support new findings.

04

Freshness and confidence

Every record carries an access date and temporal scope. Confidence is assigned to a supported finding after evidence review; it is not inherited from a publisher name or registry status.

05

Scope and limitations

Each source is bounded by supported claim types, geographic scope, temporal scope, ontology layers, and stated use limitations. General policy or industry material cannot establish entity-level exposure by itself.

06

Conflict and uncertainty

Credible conflicts are disclosed rather than averaged away. Stale, incomplete, or conflicting evidence can reduce confidence or require an insufficient finding.

07

Expanded registry

The registry contains seventy-three official or high-quality records spanning controls, semiconductor policy, company filings, technical standards, HBM technical disclosures, fabrication, packaging, interconnect, power and thermal constraints, and deployment context. HBM Evidence Releases 001 and 002 added twenty-four records, including company filings, on HBM4, its base die and accelerator integration. Evidence Release 003 added nine records on advanced packaging, chiplet integration and the UCIe interface. Evidence Release 004 added five on host-interface and scale-out networking specifications. Evidence Release 005 added eight on rack power, cooling architecture and facility metrics. Evidence Release 006 added three on installation, bring-up and operations procedures. The IDE-008 maintenance audit added four found while rechecking conflicts: the Vera Rubin NVL72 specification page, the MI300X data sheet and the current eCFR text of two HBM export-control sections. Two remain pending review and are not treated as active evidence.

08

Evidence maintenance

Inspected: 2026-11-10An inspection checks a source record and whether its quoted passages are still published. It is not a review of the claims those passages support, and no human scientific review is recorded for any source. Access dates in the registry are not changed by an inspection.

Metadata checks on every registry record. Retrieval of each canonical URL (and listed pages of multi-page documents) with a declared, honest user agent; one attempt per URL, no browser impersonation. Text extracted from HTML, or from PDF in reading-order and layout modes, and searched for every passage quoted by release findings, with recorded fallbacks for hyphenation and typographic marks.

Dates are kept apart. A source has a publication or effective date, the date it was accessed for the claims it supports, and the date of each inspection. A claim is assessed in its own release, and scientific review would be a further date that no record yet carries. An old publication date does not make a historical claim wrong. A recent one does not make a figure suitable for a current comparison.
